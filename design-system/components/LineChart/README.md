A time series, built as the **emphasis** form rather than plain multi-categorical: one series in `chart-pace` (the story), one in `chart-other` (the comparison, grayed). Reach for this pattern — not a flat multi-line chart — any time one line is the point and the rest is context (this week vs. last week, this run vs. your average). For two genuinely equal series, use two full `chart-*` colors instead and keep `chart-other` for a true "everything else" case.

**Never give this chart a second y-axis.** Two measures of different scale (pace and heart rate, say) are two separate line charts, small multiples, or indexed to a common base — not one chart with two scales. This card intentionally compares two pace series (same unit) so a single axis always tells the truth.

The axis direction is deliberate: pace is "lower is better," so the y-axis is **not** inverted the way a normal numeric axis would be — a faster time sits higher on the chart, matching how a reader expects "improving" to look. Say so in the caption whenever a pace or time axis isn't in plain ascending order; don't make a reader infer it.

**The caption states a finding, not just the methodology.** "This week vs. last week · min/mi" describes the chart but tells a reader nothing the chart doesn't already show faster than reading it would. Pull the one fact from the data that's actually worth a sentence — this card's caption names the week's fastest day and by how much — and fold the axis-direction note onto the end of that sentence rather than giving it the whole caption to itself. A chart with no real finding that week still earns a caption, just an honest one ("No day beat last week's pace" is a finding too); it never defaults back to a bare axis-units label.

**Interaction, matching the system's dataviz rules exactly:** a vertical crosshair snaps to the nearest day (seven generous hit columns, not a 2px line to aim at), one tooltip lists every series at that x with the value leading and the series name secondary, and the same thing happens on keyboard focus as on hover (tab through the hit columns). The end of each line carries a direct label — `ink` for the series that's the point, `ink-secondary` for the context line — never the series color on the text itself. **"View as table" is not decorative**: it's the WCAG-equivalent twin required by the system's dataviz rules, built from the exact same data, and it's how this chart stays usable with a screen reader or without a mouse at all.

Reuse the `.chart-card`, `.chart-head`, `.chart-legend`, `.chart-tooltip` and `.chart-table` classes from `bundle.css` for every chart in this system, line or otherwise — that shared chrome is what makes Line Chart, Bar Chart and Heatmap read as one family instead of three different widgets.
