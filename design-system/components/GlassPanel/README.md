The surface behind every card, sheet, chart container and the tab bar — Strydr's one material, built to match Apple's real Liquid Glass (WWDC 2025 / iOS 26) rather than a generic frosted-card look. This card has three parts: the material at rest, a true-refraction technique for one-off hero moments, and a live press demo (full interaction lives on the Motion card).

**The material.** Apply `glass` to any container. It sets a translucent `background` layering a corner `glass-specular` glint over `glass-fill`, `backdrop-filter: blur() saturate(180%) contrast(1.05)`, a 1px `glass-border`, and `shadow-glass` — a drop shadow plus a two-tone inset bevel (bright top, pooled-dark bottom via `glass-lens-dark`) standing in for a real lens rim. Add `glass--sm` over a compact chip or the tab bar, or `glass--lg` over a full-screen sheet; the default weight (`blur-md`) covers dashboard cards, the chart container, the map's floating info card. The glint is always top-left and never re-angled per instance — that consistency is what makes every panel read as the same material rather than a one-off effect.

Always give a glass panel a `radius-lg` or larger corner (`radius-xl` on sheets) — Strydr has no sharp-cornered surface. Never nest two glass panels directly against each other with no gap; stack them on `space-4` or more so each keeps its own shadow and edge. Because the fill is translucent, never place body text smaller than `body-sm` directly on glass without the fill behind it at full strength — the panel always needs *something* with contrast behind it (the page background, a photo, a map), never another near-identical glass panel. A flat, single-color background behind glass makes it read as a plain translucent box, not glass — see the color-wash backgrounds on this card and the Button card. Where `backdrop-filter` isn't supported, the panel falls back to the solid `surface-1`/`surface-2` color; where `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` is set, it does the same deliberately, and `prefers-contrast: more` swaps the glint/bevel for a plain `ink`-colored border.

**True lensing.** The default recipe above is a fast approximation — a glint and a bevel, no actual pixel-bending. The second section on this card shows the real thing: `backdrop-filter: url(#glass-lens) blur() saturate()`, referencing an inline SVG filter (`feTurbulence` → `feGaussianBlur` → `feDisplacementMap`, values in the card's markup) that genuinely warps whatever sits behind the panel, the way a real lens bends a straight line passing behind it. It's the closest a browser gets to Apple's real-time refraction. It renders reliably in Chromium, is inconsistent in Safari, and is expensive to composite — use it for exactly one surface on a screen (an onboarding card, a splash moment), never the whole dashboard; every other glass surface in the system uses the cheap default.

**Motion.** `glass-interactive` plus a `.glass-touch` child element is what the press demo on this card uses — press scales the panel to `.965` on the bouncy `--ease-glass` curve and lights up a radial glow at the exact contact point, then settles back out on release. That's the control-level interaction only; morphing between shapes and the scroll-responsive tab bar live on the Motion card.

**Where this system diverges from Apple's own rule:** Apple reserves Liquid Glass for the control/navigation layer and keeps it off content (cards, lists, media). Strydr intentionally puts glass on dashboard cards too, for the brief's "everything is glass" sporty feel — see the README's Glass section for the full reasoning and how to walk it back if this system ever needs to feel more restrained.
